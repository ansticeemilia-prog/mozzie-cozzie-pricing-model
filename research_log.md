# Data sources and assumptions

Keeping track here of what's actually confirmed vs. what I estimated, since a chunk of the model relies on numbers competitors don't publish.

Confirmed directly from product pages: Rohan's Atlas T-Shirt at £30 (rohan.co.uk), Páramo's Katmai Shirt at £100, 200g, UPF 50+ (paramo-clothing.com), Craghoppers' NosiLife Adventure Shirt in recycled nylon/polyamide and the Pro Shirt in recycled polyester/elastane (both craghoppers.com), Uniqlo's AIRism Shirt as a recycled polyester blend made in China (uniqlo.com), and Regatta's Anti-Insect Travel Light Shirt as a recycled polyester blend, UPF 50+, made in Bangladesh (regatta.com).

A few things I had to estimate rather than find published anywhere. Uniqlo's weight, around 150g, isn't listed on the product page, so I estimated it based on similar lightweight technical fabrics in the mass-market segment. The construction cost for Mozzie Cozzie's Air-Gap Design, £5 per unit, isn't a chemical treatment cost since it's a structural design feature (a gap between fabric and skin), so I added a cost for the extra pattern-cutting and sewing that would likely involve, though I don't have an external source for the figure, just a reasoned guess. Páramo's dense weave got a smaller construction cost, £2.50, on the logic that it's more a property of the fabric itself woven at the mill rather than something adding extra steps to garment construction. The fabric cost per gram for recycled nylon and recycled polyester blend, around £0.020 to £0.021, is estimated close to the existing recycled polyester figure since I couldn't find supplier pricing for those specific blends. And I used a flat overhead of £4 per unit across every brand, mainly because I had no real way to research packaging or logistics costs per brand and didn't want to guess at brand-specific numbers.

A couple of gaps I decided to leave as gaps rather than fill in. Uniqlo doesn't publish a UPF rating, so that's blank rather than invented, meaning it's excluded from the price-vs-protection chart but still shows up in the cost and margin analysis, which doesn't need UPF anyway. Rohan doesn't say where the shirt is manufactured either, so that's blank too, and it just falls back to a generic labour cost assumption in the model. And Mozzie Cozzie sells as a bundle rather than a single item, so it's excluded from the chart comparing single garments but included in the cost-bridge and margin numbers.
